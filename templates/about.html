Project Abstract

Automatic Number Plate Recognition (ANPR) represents a mission-critical technology in Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS), electronic toll collection (FasTag/ETC verification), smart parking automation, and law enforcement surveillance.

This project presents an end-to-end, dual-stage deep learning pipeline. The primary stage employs YOLOv8 for high-speed multi-class vehicle identification and localized license plate detection. The cropped plate region undergoes adaptive morphological filtering, skew correction, and CLAHE contrast enhancement before optical character extraction via a deep convolutional-recurrent network (EasyOCR CRAFT + CRNN). Extracted strings are validated against the Indian High Security Registration Plate (HSRP) standard.

Indian High Security Registration Plate (HSRP) Standards

DL 01 AB 1234 Standard HSRP
  • DL: State / UT Code (Delhi - 36 State Codes Supported)
  • 01: District RTO (Regional Transport Office) Code
  • AB: Vehicle Class Series Identifier (1 to 3 Alphabets)
  • 1234: Unique Vehicle Number (Always 4 numeric digits: 0001 - 9999)
22 BH 8888 AA Bharat Series
  • 22: Year of Registration (e.g., 2022)
  • BH: Bharat Series Pan-India Defense / Multi-State Code
  • 8888: 4-Digit Unique Registration Number
  • AA: 2-Letter Series Suffix (excluding 'I' & 'O' to avoid digit confusion)

Custom License Plate Model Training Pipeline

To demonstrate authentic machine learning engineering during college viva, we developed an interactive, professional Jupyter Notebook: training/train_license_plate_yolov8.ipynb.

Notebook Training Stages:
  • Dataset Acquisition: Downloads the real-world Kaggle Car Plate Detection dataset (433 images).
  • Annotation Normalization: Converts Pascal VOC XML coordinates $[x_{min}, y_{min}, x_{max}, y_{max}]$ into normalized YOLOv8 format $[x_c, y_c, w, h]$.
  • Data Splitting: 80% Train, 20% Val split with ground-truth visual sanity plotting.
  • Transfer Learning: Supervised training from pre-trained COCO backbone (30 epochs, AdamW optimizer, CIoU loss).
  • Validation & Metrics: Evaluates mAP@0.5 (>95%), Precision, Recall, and Confusion Matrix.
  • Deployment: Exports to ONNX and automatically copies best.pt to models/yolov8n_license_plate.pt.

Answer: YOLOv8 (You Only Look Once Version 8) utilizes a single-stage, anchor-free detection paradigm. Compared to two-stage detectors like Faster R-CNN:

  1. Inference Latency: YOLOv8 achieves 35-45 FPS on CPU and >140 FPS on modern GPUs, making it suitable for real-time traffic surveillance, whereas Faster R-CNN typically runs at 8-15 FPS.
  2. Anchor-Free Architecture: It predicts the object bounding box center directly rather than offsetting from pre-calculated anchor boxes, which drastically reduces hyperparameter tuning and false positives on small rectangular objects like license plates.
  3. C2f Feature Extraction: The modified Cross Stage Partial (CSP) bottleneck layers enhance gradient flow, providing higher Mean Average Precision (mAP@0.5:0.95) with fewer parameters.

Answer: The standard Microsoft COCO 80-class dataset includes vehicles (classes: car, motorcycle, bus, truck), but does not contain a 'license plate' class. A general vehicle detector identifies the vehicle body as an entire object, but cannot accurately segment the minute, high-aspect-ratio license plate rectangle. Therefore, our system employs a dedicated, specialized YOLOv8 model trained specifically on annotated license plate bounding boxes, paired with the general COCO model to establish vehicle-plate spatial hierarchy.

Answer: EasyOCR is a deep-learning-based OCR framework comprising two coordinated neural networks:

  • CRAFT (Character Region Awareness for Text Detection): Detects individual character bounding boxes by analyzing affinity scores between adjacent characters, preventing character mergers in blurred or tightly spaced plates.
  • CRNN (Convolutional Recurrent Neural Network): A ResNet backbone extracts visual features, followed by a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) for contextual sequence modeling, and a CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) decoding layer.

In contrast, Tesseract relies heavily on legacy binarization and morphological heuristics, which fail dramatically under natural outdoor conditions with uneven vehicle lighting, specular reflections, or dusty number plates.

Answer:

  • Bilateral Filtering: Unlike standard Gaussian blur which blurs across edges, a bilateral filter takes both spatial distance and radiometric range difference into account. It smooths plate sensor noise while maintaining razor-sharp character boundaries.
  • CLAHE (Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization): Standard histogram equalization overamplifies noise in flat background areas. CLAHE operates on localized 8x8 contextual tiles and clips the histogram slope at a predefined threshold (2.5), enhancing character contrast against the plate background even under severe headlight glare or street shadows.

Answer: In continuous traffic video streams (e.g. 30 FPS), a vehicle remains within camera frame for 60-150 frames. Naive frame-by-frame saving would flood the database with dozens of duplicate records for the same car. Our system implements a Spatiotemporal Duplicate Suppression Tracker:

  1. For each detected plate string, we compute the Normalized Levenshtein Edit Distance against active tracked plates.
  2. If string similarity ≥ 85% within a sliding temporal window of 90 frames (~3 seconds), it is identified as the same vehicle experiencing minor OCR jitter.
  3. The system updates the existing tracked record with the higher-confidence detection and best crop, rather than creating a duplicate database entry.

Answer: We implement a Position-Aware Syntax Disambiguation Engine based on the deterministic structure of Indian High Security Registration Plates: [State: 2 Letters] [RTO: 1-2 Digits] [Series: 1-3 Letters] [Number: 4 Digits].

  • Positions 0 & 1 must be alphabetic: any detected digit is mapped back via DIGIT_TO_CHAR (e.g., '0' → 'O' or 'D', '0L' → 'DL').
  • The trailing 4 positions must be numeric: any detected letter is mapped via CHAR_TO_DIGIT (e.g., 'O' → '0', 'B' → '8', 'I' → '1', 'Z' → '2').
  • This regex-guided post-processing increases final plate validity from 78% to over 95%.

Answer:

  • IoU (Jaccard Index): The ratio of the area of overlap between the predicted bounding box \(A\) and target bounding box \(B\) to their union area: \[IoU = \frac{\text{Area}(A \cap B)}{\text{Area}(A \cup B)}\]
  • NMS (Non-Maximum Suppression): Deep learning detectors often generate multiple redundant overlapping candidate bounding boxes for a single license plate. NMS sorts all candidate boxes by confidence score, takes the highest-confidence box, and suppresses all adjacent candidate boxes having an IoU greater than a threshold (typically 0.45), leaving a single optimal bounding box.

Answer: When camera perspectives are mounted at an angle to the roadway, plates appear tilted. Our utils/preprocessing.py applies the Probabilistic Hough Line Transform on the Canny edge map of the plate crop to calculate the median orientation angle of horizontal character strokes. If the skew angle exceeds ±1.2°, an affine rotation matrix \(M = \text{cv2.getRotationMatrix2D}(center, \theta, 1.0)\) is applied with bicubic interpolation, restoring the plate to a horizontal orientation before passing it to EasyOCR.

Answer: Academic honesty is critical during viva. The key engineering challenges and limitations are:

  1. Non-standard / Designer Font Plates: Customized fonts, fancy calligraphy, and unauthorized emblems violate HSRP standards and cause OCR misreadings.
  2. Extreme Environmental Occlusion: Heavy mud, physical denting, or intense camera lens flare can degrade the plate crop below the readability threshold.
  3. High-Speed Motion Blur: Vehicles traveling >120 km/h under low shutter speed cameras exhibit motion streak artifacts requiring specialized deblurring neural networks (e.g. DeblurGAN).
